## Courses from HR associations, universities, and training providers

These structured introductory courses come from established HR and academic institutions. They’re credible and well organized, with varying amounts of hands-on HR practice.

### 2. SHRM Essentials of Human Resources

Best for: Beginners who prefer a live, instructor-led class.

SHRM Essentials of Human Resources is a live online, instructor-led seminar for people who are new to HR or want a structured refresher. It covers talent acquisition and onboarding; compensation, benefits, and payroll; employment law and workplace compliance; performance management and employee relations; and HR’s role in supporting business strategy. You work through real scenarios, and the seminar includes access to an online companion course. It earns 15 SHRM PDCs.

#### What to consider:

- Schedule: The seminar runs live on set dates, so you’ll fit your learning around SHRM’s timetable.

- Cost: $1,890, with SHRM members saving $325.

- Depth: It’s a broad overview of core HR topics in a single seminar. SHRM positions it as preparation for more advanced HR training.

### 3. Human Resource Management: HR for People Managers (Coursera)

Best for: A college-level overview of HR from a manager’s point of view.

This five-course specialization from the University of Minnesota covers preparing to manage HR; recruiting, hiring, and onboarding; managing employee performance; and managing compensation. A final capstone has you identify HR challenges in a workplace, which can be your own, and build an action plan to address them. It’s rated beginner level, needs no prior experience, and takes about two months at 10 hours a week. You earn a shareable certificate from the University of Minnesota.

#### What to consider:

- Written for people managers: As the title suggests, it’s built around managing people. It’s also open to people considering an HR career, but it isn’t designed around the day-to-day tasks of an HR coordinator or generalist.

- Academic style: Coursera describes it as college-level, so expect a more academic approach.

- Cost: You need a paid enrollment or a Coursera Plus subscription ($239 a year). Financial aid may be available.

- Credits: The certificate doesn’t carry university credit, and the course page doesn’t mention HR recertification credits.



## Formal HR qualifications

### 4. CIPD Level 3 Foundation Certificate in People Practice

Best for: People in the U.K. who want a CIPD qualification and Associate membership.

The CIPD Foundation Certificate gives you a grounding in all areas of HR. It covers business, culture, and change in context; principles of analytics; core behaviors for people professionals; and essentials of people practice. It suits HR administrators, people moving into HR, and non-HR managers with people responsibilities. It leads to CIPD Associate membership.

#### What to consider:

- Time and assessment: It’s an academic qualification assessed through written assignments. One U.K. college, for example, runs it over eight months with three to four hours of study a week.

- Cost: At that college, the course costs £1,300, plus a £40 CIPD joining fee and a £99 student membership fee. Prices vary by provider.

- Entry requirements: Some providers ask for GCSEs or relevant work experience.

- Global fit: It’s most valued by U.K. employers. If you’re a CIPD member, you can also record AIHR learning as verified CPD.

## Short introductory courses

These are low-cost ways to test your interest in HR before you commit to a longer program.

### 5. HR Fundamentals (FutureLearn)

Best for: Testing whether HR is right for you.

Created by the CIPD and hosted on FutureLearn, this self-paced course runs about five weeks at around three hours a week. It covers what HR is, HR professionalism, people and strategy, recruitment and retention, and performance, engagement, and reward.

What to consider: Free access is limited, and you pay to keep access and earn a certificate. It’s an introduction, so plan a next step if you want job-ready skills.

### 6. The Human Resources (HR) Mastery Bundle

Best for: A video-based overview of a wide range of HR topics.

Created by Expert Academy and Lahcen Bouya, this Udemy bundle of 10 courses covers HR fundamentals and workforce planning, job descriptions, hiring, remote onboarding, talent management, cross-cultural communication, supporting neurodiversity, performance management, employee retention and workplace culture, and using AI in HR. It runs to 100 video lectures (8 hours 52 minutes). The only stated requirement is an open mind, so you don’t need prior HR knowledge. Learners rate it 4.6 out of 5 from 1,148 ratings.

What to consider: It’s built around video lectures, so plan how you’ll practice what you learn. Check the current price on Udemy before you enroll.

### 7. Oxford Home Study Centre

Best for: Free, self-paced introductions to HR topics.

Oxford Home Study Center offers free online HR courses , including HR Management, Recruitment Skills, HR and AI, and Talent Acquisition Management. Each takes about 20 hours, is self-paced, and includes tutor support through a learning portal. Topics include HR fundamentals, recruitment and selection, staff development, and HR automation.

What to consider: Enrolling is free, but certificates are paid extras. Paid certificates are either CPD-accredited by the CPD Standards Office or endorsed by the Quality License Scheme, and they aren’t Ofqual-regulated qualifications.



## Webinars and continuing education

### 8. Compliance Prime

Best for: Staying current on specific compliance topics.

U.S.-based Compliance Prime runs live and on-demand webinars across HR, payroll, and other compliance areas, typically 60 to 90 minutes long. Upcoming HR sessions cover topics such as multi-state employment law, ADA accommodation requests, and Form I-9 compliance. Webinars earn continuing education credits, including SHRM and HRCI, and come with a certificate of participation.

#### What to consider:

- Cost: Memberships cost $799 for six months or $999 for a year, or you can buy a 10-webinar pack for $699.

- Focus: Webinars are single-topic sessions, mostly on U.S. compliance. They work best as a supplement once you have a structured foundation, rather than as your first step into HR.

## How to choose an HR course as a beginner

Use these six questions to compare any option, including the ones on this list.

- Can you start without HR experience? Check entry requirements. Some courses assume working knowledge of HR, and some qualifications ask for prior education.

- Will you practice the work, or only learn about it? Look for assignments, case studies, simulations, and templates. Video lectures and reading build understanding. Applying them is what builds confidence on the job.

- Is it recognized by HR bodies? Recertification credits from organizations such as SHRM, HRCI, HRPA, CPHR, ATD, and CPD UK signal that the content meets professional standards.

- Does the format fit your life? Self-paced online learning works around a job. Scheduled classes, set start dates, and live webinars need more planning.

- What’s the total cost? Add up course fees, membership fees, and subscription costs.

- Can you explore different HR areas? Many beginners don’t yet know whether they’ll specialize in recruitment, people analytics, or learning and development. Access to more than one HR domain helps you find your fit.
