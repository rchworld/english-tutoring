## What is an HR certification?

An HR certification can mean one of two things. The first is exam-based: you study, sit an exam through a reputable HR training organization, usually after gaining relevant work experience, and the credential you earn proves what you already know.

The second is a certificate program that teaches you practical skills. An AIHR Certificate Program , for instance, builds the modern, practical skills, so you can immediately apply what you have learned on the job.

Credentials and certificate programs aren’t mutually exclusive; instead, you can take both together or after each other. You can not only grow your skill set with a certificate program but also maintain your existing certification by earning credits through course completion.

For example, AIHR is recognized by SHRM to offer Professional Development Credits (PDCs) for SHRM-CP or SHRM-SCP recertification activities, and its Certificate Programs count toward recertification credits with HRCI, HRPA, CPHR, ATD, and CIPD.

So, an HR certification is, generally, a credential or certificate program that recognizes your Human Resources knowledge and skills, whether you earn it by passing an exam after gaining relevant experience or by completing an HR domain-specific training program.

## Do you need experience to get an HR certification?

Experience is not always required to earn an HR certification, and the extent you need it depends on which credential or program you choose.

Traditional, exam-based HR certifications set their own bar. HRCI’s PHR asks for professional-level HR experience through one of three paths: 1 year with a master’s degree, 2 years with a bachelor’s degree, or 4 years with no degree. SPHR requires four years of experience with a master’s degree, five years with a bachelor’s degree, or seven years with no experience.

SHRM’s Senior Certified Professional (SHRM-SCP) works the same way, requiring at least three years in a strategic-level HR role, or three years holding the SHRM-CP while you move into one.

While other exam-based certifications may not have stringent eligibility requirements. For example, HRCI’s aPHR is designed for people who haven’t worked in HR yet, and no HR experience is required. SHRM-CP doesn’t set an experience requirement either; it just expects a working knowledge of HR practices, which you can pick up through a relevant degree or through the job itself.

When an HR certification does require experience, most bodies build in alternative routes. Relevant education, such as an undergraduate or graduate degree in HR, business administration, or a related field, often substitutes for some of the required years. Internships, volunteer HR work, leadership roles in student organizations, and employer apprenticeship or career-pathing programs can all count, too, depending on the certifying body’s rules.

Certificate programs like AIHR’s often don’t have eligibility requirements. Whether you’re new to HR, moving into a specialist track, or already leading a team, you can enroll and start building the skill immediately, because the program is designed to teach you the domain rather than test whether you already know it.

If you’re early in your HR career or changing direction, you can build the specific skills you need now, through an HR certification course , instead of waiting until you’ve banked enough professional-level hours to qualify for an exam-based credential.

Once you do hold a certification, most require you to keep it current through continuing education, which you can typically earn through workshops, webinars, and further coursework rather than by retaking the original exam.

### aPHR (HRCI)

The Associate Professional in Human Resources (aPHR) is HRCI’s entry-level credential, and it’s the one HRCI certification that requires zero HR experience. aPHR certification requirements are brief: a high school diploma or equivalent is sufficient, with no work history required.

It costs $400 in total (a $100 non-refundable application fee, plus a $300 exam fee). The credential stays valid for three years, and you’ll need 45 recertification credits to keep it active.

### SHRM-CP (SHRM)

The SHRM-CP works from a different angle. SHRM doesn’t require a degree, an HR title, or previous HR experience to sit for the exam, though a working knowledge of HR practices is recommended.

Pricing is set at $399 for SHRM members and $499 for non-members, while recertification requires 60 professional development credits every three years.

### aPHRi (HRCI)

HRCI’s aPHRi certification is the aPHR equivalent for HR professionals building a career outside the U.S. It has the same zero-experience policy: a high school diploma or global equivalent is enough to apply, and no prior HR work history is required.

Like the aPHR, the exam costs $400 in total. The credential remains valid for 3 years, with 45 recertification credits required to keep it active, and the exam is also offered in Arabic for candidates testing in that market.

### Level 3 Foundation Certificate in People Practice (CIPD)

The CIPD Level 3 Foundation Certificate works differently from HRCI’s and SHRM’s single-exam model. There are no formal entry requirements beyond a minimum age (typically 16 to 19, depending on the study center) and a recommended level of literacy and numeracy. Rather than taking one proctored exam, you complete four written assignments over roughly eight to 12 months of part-time study through a CIPD-approved center.

Fees aren’t centrally fixed the way HRCI’s or SHRM’s are. According to the CIPD, the course costs between £1,300 and £2,300, plus a separate annual CIPD student membership fee of £118. At the same time, the exact final price depends on which training provider you choose. Once you finish it, you automatically get CIPD Foundation membership, the first rung of CIPD’s professional ladder.

### Fundamental Payroll Certification (PayrollOrg)

PayrollOrg’s Fundamental Payroll Certification (FPC) skips the HR Generalist path altogether and certifies payroll knowledge specifically. It has no experience or education prerequisites, and you don’t need to be a member of PayrollOrg to register.

The exam consists of 150 questions over three hours, and costs $359 for PayrollOrg members and $459 for non-members. The credential is valid for three years, and you’ll need 60 recertification credit hours to keep it active.

## 4 steps to get HR certified without experience

Gaining an HR certification without prior experience is a strategic way to enter the field of Human Resources . Here’s a roadmap to navigate this process:

### Step 1: Gain relevant HR skills

You can start building basic HR knowledge even if you have not gained formal HR experience. Be on the lookout for volunteer opportunities and internships to learn HR 101.

Consider working in customer service, administration, or any position focusing on people management and organizational skills, as these can also provide relevant experience.

### Step 2: Prepare for the certification exam

Join study groups with your peers who are also preparing for the same examination, so you get motivated and get help to understand complex topics. Use practice exams to familiarize you with the format and types of questions that will be asked in the test.

Reflect on practice test results so you’ll know what to improve. Create a study plan covering all topics discussed in the course. Use the recommended resources and materials so you are fully prepared.

### Step 3: Complete the exam application process

Be sure to meet the eligibility requirements for the certification you’re applying for, including education, certificate program completion, and sometimes work experience. Prepare all the documents, such as certificates of completion, IDs, and educational transcripts.

Follow the application process as outlined by the certifying body, which typically includes completing an application form and paying an exam fee. Monitor the status of your application.

### Step 4: Invest in continuous learning

Getting certified can help advance your HR career, but it may not be enough. HR is changing quickly, especially with AI reshaping how the function works, so staying relevant means continuing to build skills after you’ve passed your exam or finished your certificate program.

AIHR’s learning platform provides an extensive library of online training courses and mini-courses that let you build specific skills (including AI in HR) with practical online learning.

### HR tip

Once certified, maintain your credentials by attending workshops, seminars, and courses to earn the required continuing education credits. Stay updated on the renewal processes to ensure your certification is recovered.

## How to get HR experience

For aspiring HR professionals looking to gain experience and broaden their expertise in the field, the following tips can help pave the way for a successful career in Human Resources:

- Cross-skilling within your organization : Volunteer on projects or tasks outside your job, like participating in recruitment events. Look for job opportunities in payroll or ER to understand how HR impacts these departments.

- Job shadowing : You can request to shadow HR staff in your company or through your work connections to gain hands-on experience in daily HR tasks and challenges. Learn about different aspects of HR, like benefits management, talent acquisition, or compliance, so you’ll know which one you’d like to specialize in.

- Joining professional HR associations : Join esteemed human resource associations like SHRM (Society for Human Resource Management) or HRCI (HR Certification Institute) to access resources, workshops, and networking events. Be an active member by participating in seminars and conferences to build connections and stay informed about the latest industry trends and best practices.

- Applying for entry-level HR positions : Search for positions that serve as a stepping stone into the HR field, such as HR assistant or recruiter assistant roles, even if they require minimal experience. Arrange your resume to highlight relevant skills (communication skills, organizational skills) and HR-related experience like internships or volunteer work.

- Continued education : Enroll in HR certification programs or pursue a degree in human resources to gain comprehensive knowledge and credentials to enhance your resume. Attend webinars, take online courses, and read industry publications for the newest updates on the sector.

- Developing technology skills : Learn standard HR tools like application tracking systems (ATS), human resource information systems ( HRIS ), and employee performance management tools. Learn data analysis software, as HR is now gearing towards making data-driven decisions.
