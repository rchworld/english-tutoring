## What to look for in an HR Generalist certification

Your week might include recruitment planning on Monday, payroll questions Wednesday, and an employee concern Friday, so your training should reflect that range. Use these five criteria to compare your options:

- Employee life cycle coverage: Check for recruitment, onboarding, performance, rewards, employee relations, and offboarding. Employee relations covers workplace relationships and concerns. Offboarding covers the steps you take when someone leaves.

- Eligibility: Some credentials require professional HR experience, while others accept beginners. Check that you qualify before setting your budget.

- Recognition: SHRM and HRCI exam credentials from assess HR knowledge, and use published standards. Certificate programs, on the other hand, confirm that you’ve completed structured training and met the provider’s assessment requirements.

- Practical application: Look for projects, case studies, and templates you can adapt at work. Examples include a policy framework and an onboarding checklist.

- Cost, time, and upkeep: Add exam or tuition fees, study time, and preparation materials, then check the requirements and costs for keeping the credential active.

No option will meet every need equally. An exam credential may help when employers name it in job postings, while project-based certificate program can help you build skills and work samples. Choose the criteria that matter most for your next role.

### SHRM Certified Professional (SHRM)

The SHRM-CP assesses how you apply HR knowledge in day-to-day work, which includes putting policies into practice and serving as employees’ HR contact.

- Best for: Generalists who want a competency-based credential and a path toward the senior SHRM-SCP.

- Eligibility: No HR title, degree, or prior HR experience required, though SHRM recommends a basic working knowledge of HR.

- Format and duration: 134 multiple-choice questions that mix knowledge items with situational judgment scenarios. The exam lasts three hours and 40 minutes, and is held in person at a Prometric test center.

- Cost: $399 for SHRM members and $499 for non-members.

- What to consider: The exam measures what you know rather than teaching you how to do the work, so you’ll need to plan separate preparation and hands-on practice. You can also only test during SHRM’s two yearly windows, so be sure to check the SHRM website to determine when you can register.

### Professional in Human Resources (HRCI)

HRCI’s PHR focuses on technical and operational HR knowledge, including U.S. laws and regulations.

- Best for: Experienced generalists whose work centers on compliance, employee relations, and HR administration.

- Eligibility: One year of professional-level HR experience with a master’s degree, two years with a bachelor’s degree, or four years with no degree.

- Format and duration: Two-hour exam; available year-round.

- Cost: $495, which includes a $100 application fee and a $395 exam fee.

- What to consider: You’ll need professional HR experience before you can apply, so the PHR isn’t an option if you’re new to HR.

## HR Generalist certifications outside the U.S.

Be sure to check the geographic focus before enrolling. HRCI offers the PHRi, an international version of its PHR, at the same fees you’d pay for the PHR. The SHRM-CP also serves international candidates. Their exams may cover general employment law concepts rather than a specific country’s laws.

Professional bodies also vary by location. Examples include the Chartered Institute of Personnel and Development (CIPD) in the U.K. and Ireland. In Canada, explore Chartered Professionals in Human Resources (CPHR). Check local job postings to see which credentials employers request.

AIHR offers eligible learning for SHRM, HRCI, HRPA, CPHR, and the Association for Talent Development (ATD). Its CPD Certification Service-approved learning can count toward CIPD members’ continuing professional development (CPD). Check each body’s requirements.
