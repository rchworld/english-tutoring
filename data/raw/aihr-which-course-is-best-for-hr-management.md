## Skills you need to build for Human Resource Management

What future-ready HR skills do you need to build to succeed in an HR management role? The answer depends on multiple variables, such as your current skill set, experience, potential skill gaps, and growth ambitions, but examples include:

- HR management knowledge and expertise: Every aspiring HR professional needs to know the basics of Strategic Human Resource Management and the key HR functions .

- HR strategy creation and execution: As Dr. Dieter Veldsman, Chief HR Scientist at AIHR, puts it: “The HR strategy clarifies how HR will contribute to achieving the business objectives and helps to guide all HR activities.” Knowing how to develop an effective HR strategy and how to execute it is a skill HRM practitioners need to master.

- Stakeholder management: Building and maintaining relationships with employees, senior leadership, managers, and other stakeholders is an essential part of everyday HR Management.

- AI fluency: AI fluency is becoming a baseline HR competency . AI fluency is becoming a baseline HR competency. AI-fluent HR professionals apply AI confidently and responsibly, supporting its adoption across HR and the broader business, and integrate AI into everyday work in ways that strengthen processes and outcomes.

- People management skills: HR Managers need effective people management skills , including giving and receiving feedback, problem-solving, empathy, and effective communication. These skills are essential for effectively leading HR teams, interacting with diverse stakeholders, and performing various HR functions.

- Project management: HR project management skills are indispensable for every HR Manager. Whether implementing a new HRIS , optimizing onboarding programs, or rolling out a salary review cycle, understanding how to apply project management techniques enables HR teams to execute their plans efficiently and effectively.

- Resilience: According to the recent HR Mental Wellbeing Report , 87% of HR professionals report not feeling supported at work, and 63% say they are ‘very likely’ to experience burnout. These numbers have been worryingly high for the past years and emphasize the importance of HR practitioners working on building resilience and prioritizing their overall wellbeing. In other words, HR needs to start practicing HR for HR , and managers should set the right example.

### 4. Fundamentals of Human Resources Management – AMA

The Fundamentals of Human Resources Management course is a two-day seminar, offered in person, live online, or at your company’s location, for $2,745 (or $2,495 for AMA members). It covers the core HR functions and topics such as:

- Performance management

- Coaching and leadership development

- Legal responsibilities

- And more.

Keep in mind: This is a fundamentals course, so it focuses mainly on essential HR functions rather than HR strategy or business alignment. Its legal content covers U.S. federal employment legislation, and as a scheduled two-day seminar, you’ll need to block out full days on set dates rather than learn at your own pace.

## 6 steps to choose the best HR Management course for you

So, how do you decide which of the Human Resource Management courses is best for you? As mentioned above, multiple factors play a role here. Some steps to think about:

- Start with the role you want next: If you’re aiming for an HR Manager role, look for a program built around how HR creates business value, not just HR basics. The strongest programs teach you to connect HR objectives to business goals, build and execute an HR strategy, and think like a business leader. These are the skills that earn HR a seat at the table.

- Assess your skill gaps: To become a successful HR Manager , you’ll need to master specific skills. Some of those skills we’ve discussed earlier. To find others, compare your current skills with the role you want using an HR Career Map and an HR skills analyzer . Then choose a course that closes those specific gaps.

- Check that the learning is practical: Look beyond the topic list. Strong programs include real-world case studies, hands-on assignments, and ready-to-use templates, so you can apply what you learn at work straight away. A final project that puts you in a realistic business scenario is a good sign that you’ll leave with skills, not just theory.

- Look at who built the course and what learners say: Courses designed by experienced HR practitioners tend to be easier to apply in your day-to-day work. Check verified reviews, and if possible, contact people who have taken the course. On LinkedIn, people regularly share when they’ve completed a course, making it easy to ask whether they’d recommend it.

- Consider practicalities (time, budget, format, credits): Self-paced online learning fits around a full-time job, while live seminars require you to block out whole days. Compare the cost against what you get, check how long you keep access to the materials, and, if you already hold a certification such as SHRM-CP or PHR, check whether the course earns recertification credits.

- Think beyond one course: HR management keeps evolving, especially with AI. Choose a provider that covers AI in HR, keeps its content up to date, and lets you keep building skills across other HR areas as your career grows. A community of peers you can turn to for support and ideas is a bonus, and some providers let you preview lessons before you commit.

## FAQ

Which course is best for HR management? For HR professionals who want practical, self-paced learning they can apply at work, AIHR’s HR Manager Certificate Program covers HR strategy, organizational design, HR operating models, and business acumen, and earns recertification credits with SHRM, HRCI, and HRPA. The best course for you depends on your experience and goals, shorter introductory courses suit those just starting out, while certifications such as SHRM-CP or CIPD validate existing expertise.

What qualifications do I need to be an HR professional? Usually, organizations ask for a Bachelor’s degree in HRM or a related field. Sometimes they also require additional HR qualifications, so if you have taken, for example, an HR Management course, this can be advantageous for your chances of getting the job.

What is the most respected HR certification? Recognition depends on your region and career stage. In the U.S., SHRM-CP, SHRM-SCP, and the HRCI certifications (aPHR, PHR, SPHR) are widely recognized, while CIPD is the common standard across the UK, Ireland, and parts of Asia and Africa.
