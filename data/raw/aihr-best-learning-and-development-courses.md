## What makes a learning and development course worth it?

Use these five criteria:

- Format and flexibility: Self-paced online courses fit around a job, while cohort-based or classroom formats add structure and deadlines.

- Depth versus breadth: Some courses offer a quick overview, while others delve deeper into one area, such as instructional design or learning analytics.

- Recognition: Does the course lead to a credential that employers know? Or does it simply confirm completion? Each has value, but they serve different goals.

- Application: Strong courses include projects, templates, or case studies you can use at work.

- Cost and time: Look beyond the headline price. Check study hours, prep materials, exam fees, and renewal costs.

No single course wins on all five. A short marketplace course often scores high on flexibility and cost, but may score lower on depth and recognition. At the same time, an exam-based credential usually carries more recognition, but requires more of your time. Knowing which criteria matter most is the real decision.

## 5 key skills the best L&D courses should build

No matter which route you choose, you should judge a course by the skills it can help you develop. Modern L&D work depends on a few core capabilities, and strong courses can help build these.

### 1. Learning strategy

Align learning programs with business priorities to ensure L&D supports measurable outcomes. A strong course should help you identify what the organization needs, set learning priorities, and connect training to performance goals . Look for content that covers stakeholder alignment, learning roadmaps, and how to decide which learning initiatives deserve investment.

### 2. Instructional and learning design

Create effective learning experiences using evidence-based principles, including digital and blended formats. The course should teach you how to structure content, choose the right format, and design activities that help employees apply what they learn. Strong options also cover learner personas, accessibility, engagement , and the process of turning business needs into clear learning objectives.

### 3. Skills gap analysis

Identify the gap between current and required skills so you can direct learning where it has the greatest impact. A useful course should show you how to collect skills data, compare it against role requirements, and prioritize the most urgent gaps. It should also help you translate those gaps into practical learning plans, rather than broad training requests.

### 4. Learning analytics

Measure training effectiveness and show the business impact of L&D. Look for courses that explain how to choose learning metrics, track progress, and interpret results in a way leaders can understand. Strong courses also cover evaluation models, such as the Kirkpatrick Model, and show how to report outcomes without overclaiming impact.

### 5. Coaching

Structured coaching approaches can improve performance and leadership skills. A strong coaching course should help you ask better questions, guide development conversations, and support managers without taking over their role. It should also explain when coaching works best and when employees need training, feedback, or performance support instead.

These skills closely align with real L&D work and help HR professionals move from training delivery to strategic capability-building. If you want to go deeper before choosing a course, AIHR’s guides on learning and development , L&D strategy , and skills gap analysis are useful starting points. A course that builds several of these skills will serve you longer than one that covers a single tool or trend.
