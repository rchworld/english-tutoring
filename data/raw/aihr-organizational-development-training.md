## The benefits of organizational development training

OD training helps you build the skills and confidence to lead meaningful change. Whether you’re an HR practitioner, manager, or consultant, it helps you make a measurable impact on your organization and your own career. Here are some key benefits of organizational development training:

- Develop practical OD skills: These include the ability to assess organizational health, find performance gaps, and design interventions that improve systems, culture, and results.

- Think and act strategically: You can shift from reactive tasks to proactive influence, and align people, processes, and strategy so every initiative supports business goals.

- Accelerate your career growth: Expertise in change, culture, and people development opens doors to promotions, leadership, and roles in transformation or consulting. The U.S. Bureau of Labor Statistics projects employment of training and development managers to grow 6% from 2025 to 2035, faster than the average for all occupations.

- Increase your earning potential: Employers value HR professionals who link people strategy to business outcomes. Training and development managers, a role that often leads OD work, earned a median salary of $133,000 in May 2025.

- Gain a credible qualification: A recognized certificate shows your commitment to professional growth and builds trust with employers and clients, because it shows you can lead change with a structured, evidence-based approach.

- Expand your professional network: Many programs include cohorts, peer coaching, or alumni groups, which give you channels for ideas, advice, and networking opportunities that can help potential employers notice you.

- Lead with confidence: OD upskilling helps you guide teams through uncertainty, manage resistance, and sustain change. When you understand the human side of transformation, you can build collaboration, trust, and accountability.

### Change management programs

- The program covers: The ATD CHANGE model, systems thinking, stakeholder buy-in, building a change management team, and tools to evaluate change.

- Prerequisites: None

- Cost: $2,125 for members, $2,425 for non-members.

- Provider: UCSC Silicon Valley Extension

- Credential type: Course credit (2.0 quarter units)

- Format and duration: Live online

- The program covers: OD history and overview, theories and practices of OD, the OD process and models, and strategic partnerships between HR, management, executives, and consultants.

- Prerequisites: None

- Cost: $710

- Find out more: Organization Development and Change

- Keep in mind: It’s a single live online course, so it works as an introduction rather than a complete program, and you’ll need to attend at set times.

#### 11. Change Management Certification Program

- Provider: Prosci

- Best for: HR practitioners responsible for driving change who want a research-based methodology.

- Credential type: Prosci Certified Change Practitioner (does not expire; no mandatory recertification)

- Format and duration: Online or in person; three-day intensive program.

- The program covers: The ADKAR Model and the Prosci 3-Phase Process, applied to your real change initiatives.

- Prerequisites: None; no prior change management experience required.

- Cost: $4,500 online, $4,850 in person.

- Find out more: Change Management Certification Program

- Keep in mind: It teaches one proprietary methodology, so it’s narrower than broad OD training, and $4,500 or more is a significant spend for a three-day program.

#### 12. Certified Change Management Professional (CCMP)

- Provider: Association of Change Management Professionals (ACMP)

- Best for: Experienced change practitioners who want an exam-based professional credential.

- Credential type: Professional certification (CCMP designation, exam-based)

- Format and duration: Online via remote proctoring or at an approved testing center; three-hour multiple-choice exam (150 questions).

- The program covers: The ACMP Standard for Change Management , change theories and frameworks, and evaluating change impact and organizational readiness.

- Prerequisites: A four-year degree plus three years (4,200 hours) of change management experience, or secondary education plus five years (7,000 hours), and 21 hours of qualifying change management training.

- Cost: $595 for members, $795 for non-members.

- Find out more: Certified Change Management Professional (CCMP)

- Keep in mind: The CCMP requires years of change management experience, so you’ll still need separate training to build the relevant skills.

## How to choose the right organizational development certification

The right OD certification for you depends on what you need it to do, and whether you’ll be able to finish and apply it. Before you compare prestige or price, check each program against the following criteria.

### Practical skills you can apply right away

Look for a program built around the full OD process: Diagnosing issues, planning interventions, and measuring results. The strongest options include templates, case studies, and projects or assignments based on real-world scenarios, so you finish with work you can apply to your role or discuss with prospective employers.

### Recertification credits

If you hold a SHRM or HRCI certification, choose a program that earns SHRM PDCs and HRCI recertification credits, such as AIHR’s Organizational Development Certificate Program. Your OD training then counts toward keeping your credential active, so the same hours benefit you in two ways.

### Self-paced learning you can start anytime

Fixed cohorts, live sessions, and semester schedules work for some people, but they’re hard to fit around a full-time HR role. A self-paced online program lets you start when you’re ready, study in short blocks, and revisit lessons when you need to. AIHR’s OD program, for instance, lets you complete around 40 hours of study at your own pace over a period of 12 months.

### Up-to-date content

OD practice keeps shifting, from hybrid teams to AI-driven restructuring. Check when the curriculum was last updated, and whether it covers current topics such as data-informed diagnostics and organizational design for new ways of working. Programs that refresh their content regularly keep your skills relevant long after you finish.

### Clear value for the price

Costs range from a few hundred dollars for a single course to more than $20,000 for a graduate certificate. Compare what you get for the money: the depth of the curriculum, how long you have access, the resources included, and the credits you earn. A $1,000 program that combines a full OD curriculum with recertification credits may deliver more practical value than either a short course or costly academic program.

### When a designation or academic credit matters more

If you plan to practice as an external OD consultant, you may need a named designation such as IOD’s ODCP. If you want credits that stack toward a master’s degree, a university graduate certificate is the better fit. For most HR professionals moving into OD, though, a practical, self-paced program that meets all five criteria is the most direct route.
