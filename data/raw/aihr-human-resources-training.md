## How to choose the right HR training program

Before you choose a Human Resources training program, it’s good to take a couple of elements into consideration. Not every program will suit your role, your organization, or the way you learn. Weigh these six factors before you enroll:

### 1. Do you want practical skills or theoretical knowledge?

Theory-led programs explain why HR practices work, drawing on research and academic models. They suit you if you want a broad foundation or plan to keep studying. Practice-led training programs focus on how to do the work. You learn a method, see it applied, and then use it on a realistic task.

Look at what you’ll do and produce during the HR training program. Videos and quizzes help you understand a topic. Assignments, roleplays, case studies, templates, and projects help you apply it. If most of a program is reading and watching, it leans toward theory. You can also see some popular HR training topics below:

AIHR’s Certificate Programs are practice-led. Lessons follow the Tell-Show-Do-Apply method, so you practice through assignments, roleplays, and real-world case studies, and download templates you can use at work. Each program ends with a capstone project that puts you in a realistic scenario.

### 2. Does it cover the HR area you want to grow in?

Unless you have unlimited time and budget for learning and development , you want to start with an HR training that serves you in your day-to-day activities.

Looking at where you’re at in your career and your goals will help you focus on the subject of your training. As someone new to the field, you may look at HR training courses for beginners, while seasoned HR practitioners might want to upskill in people analytics or get an HR Manager certification.

Check that the catalog goes beyond HR fundamentals into the areas HR is moving toward, like AI, people analytics , and strategic business partnering. For example, AIHR focuses only on HR. Its 16+ certificate programs run from HR Generalist and HR Coordinator for career starters to People Analytics, Artificial Intelligence for HR, and HR Business Partner 2.0 for specialists.

Also ask how often the content is updated. HR practice and technology change quickly. With AIHR’s Full Academy Access , new courses and content released during your membership are added automatically.

### 3. Who’s teaching: Practitioners or academics?

Another aspect associated with the training subject is the person who delivers the training. Before signing up for a Human Resources training program, make sure to check out who’s teaching.

Academics bring research depth. Practitioners teach from the work they do, which keeps lessons close to the problems you’ll face on the job. If you want practical skills, look for instructors who still work in HR. AIHR’s 45+ instructors are practitioners working in the field.

See if you can watch or listen to an excerpt from the course you’re interested in to understand their teaching style and voice. Also, look at their experience in the field and what people who’ve already done one of their courses say about their teaching. With AIHR, you can watch sample lessons in the Demo Portal before you enroll.

### 4. Does it fit around your job?

Few HR professionals have an hour-long learning window every day, but most have 15 or 20 minutes here and there. Look for self-paced, bite-sized modules you can pick up between meetings, revisit before you apply them, and open on any device.

### 5. Will you learn alongside other HR professionals?

Especially for online HR training courses, it can be a real added value to have a community of fellow students. This kind of practitioner community is great if you want to share experiences, ask questions, or solve problems together with others who are on the same journey. It can also be a good place to find inspiration and an expert perspective.

AIHR members join a global community of 25,000+ HR professionals, where you can share approaches to your assignments and compare notes with peers. Full Academy Access also includes weekly live webinars with HR experts.

### 6. Will it count toward your recertification?

If you already hold a credential, check whether a program counts toward renewal before you enroll. This way, a single investment builds a skill and keeps your certification active at the same time. Look for the provider’s own statement of recognition, not just a logo on the page.

For example, AIHR is recognized by SHRM to offer Professional Development Credits (PDCs) for SHRM-CP or SHRM-SCP recertification activities. Its programs are also eligible for recertification credits with HRCI , HRPA, CPHR, and ATD, and CIPD members can record AIHR programs as verified CPD.



## Offline vs. online HR training

When choosing a course, it’s important to consider the mode of training: offline or online. One benefit of offline, on-site training is that you get to ask your questions face-to-face with (most of the time) an instant answer. You also get to meet your fellow students in person. Offline, on-site HR training also has the advantage of being less casual. You know what day(s) you have to be where and from what time.

Online HR training is a great choice for those who value flexibility and the ability to self-pace their learning. Typically, a well-thought-out course will do its best to replicate the community aspects of on-site learning, as well as teacher interactions.

The biggest advantage of online training is that it fits around your job. You can learn in 20-minute blocks, replay a module right before you apply it, and start the week you enroll instead of waiting for the next class date. The trade-off is accountability. With no fixed date, it’s easy to stall, so pick a program with projects or a community that keeps you moving.

There’s also a middle option. Live online sessions give you a set schedule and a real instructor without the travel. Hybrid formats, such as AIHR Boot Camps , pair self-paced content with instructor-led sessions.

In addition to budget and timeline, think through what environment you learn best in and choose the one that fits your learning preferences the most.

## HR training for teams

Upskilling one HR professional improves a single role, but upskilling the whole HR team changes how the function performs. A team that trains together builds a shared language, consistent ways of working, and the collective capability to deliver on business priorities like AI adoption, analytics, and strategic business partnering.

To upskill your HR team, start with a skills audit against a framework like AIHR’s T-Shaped HR Competency Model . Pick the two or three capabilities that matter most for the year ahead, such as AI adoption or business partnering. Then, match the format to the goal: team licenses suit individual learning paths, while cohort programs suit shared priorities where everyone needs the same skill at the same time.
