## Why get a recruiting certification

When you’re deciding how to invest in your recruiting career , it helps to know what each type of HR certification and course does. Recruiters can earn an exam-based certification from a body such as SHRM or AIRS, which validates knowledge you already have, or complete a certificate program from a provider such as Academy to Innovate HR (AIHR), which builds new, practical skills you can use on the job right away.

The two work well together, but if you want to get better at the work itself, a certificate program is the place to start. A strong recruiting certificate program gives you five things:

- Practical skills you can use right away: Programs built around real recruiting tasks, such as running an intake meeting or building a sourcing strategy , help you improve the work you do every week rather than just pass an exam.

- Current, in-demand methods: Hiring practices change quickly, so a recent program should cover how recruiters now use AI, structured interviewing, and recruitment data, not only the fundamentals.

- Experienced practitioners as instructors: When the people teaching have run recruiting teams and filled hard roles themselves, you learn approaches that hold up with real hiring managers and candidates.

- Credibility and career mobility: A recognized credential shows you know current hiring practice, which helps most when you’re building your case for a senior recruiter or TA partner role.

- Recertification credits: If you already hold a SHRM or HRCI certification, many recruiting programs count toward your renewal, so one investment serves two purposes.

## Best recruiting certifications

A recruiting certification is a credential offered by professional institutions or organizations to confirm that the recruiter has the requisite knowledge and skills to perform their job. You need to pass an exam to earn a certification, and it typically also requires ongoing education and periodic renewal.

### 1. Talent Acquisition Specialty Credential (SHRM)

#### Program overview

The SHRM Talent Acquisition Specialty Credential package includes the Talent Acquisition: Creating Your Organization’s Strategy program, offered in person and virtually, plus six SHRM eLearning courses on topics like global hiring and the virtual talent acquisition lifecycle.

To earn the credential, you must pass a 30-question online knowledge assessment, and you don’t have to be SHRM-certified to earn it. If you already hold the SHRM-CP or SHRM-SCP, completing the package earns you 25 PDCs toward recertification.

#### Details

- Whom it’s for: In-house recruiters and talent acquisition professionals who want a recruiter-specific credential from a widely recognized HR body.

- Format/duration: Live program (in person or virtual), self-paced eLearning, and an online assessment.

- Cost: $1,830 for SHRM members and $2,205 for non-members.

### 2. Professional Recruiter (ADP AIRS)

#### Program overview

AIRS, ADP’s recruitment training arm, offers some of the most widely recognized recruiter-specific certifications. Its flagship Professional Recruiter Certification (PRC) has been redesigned around strategic recruiting (e.g.,using labor market intelligence, building proactive pipelines, and using AI tools for sourcing and outreach.

AIRS also offers specialty certifications, including:

- Certified Associate Recruiter (CAR): For recruiters at the start of their careers.

- Certified Internet Recruiter (CIR) and Advanced CIR (ACIR): Advanced internet sourcing through AIRS SearchLab.

- Certified Technical Recruiter (CTR): Recruiting for IT and technical roles

- Certified AI and Sourcing Recruiter (CASR): Using AI in sourcing and recruiting.

- Certified Diversity Recruiter (CDR) and Certified Military Veteran Recruiter (CMVR): Building inclusive and veteran hiring programs

AIRS certifications stay valid for two years. After that, you can retake the assessment through your MyAIRS account to recertify at no extra cost.

#### Details

- Whom it’s for: Corporate and agency recruiters, TA specialists, and recruiting managers who want a recruiting-specific credential.

- Format/duration: On-demand and instructor-led options, with 12 months of access to training and coaching.

- Recertification credits: 6.5 SHRM PDCs and 6.5 HRCI recertification credits.

- Cost: $895 at list price. Recruiter Academy Premium , which includes 20+ courses and all AIRS certifications, costs $2,495 for two years of access.

### 3. Certified People Sourcing Professional (Sourcing Certifications)

#### Program overview

Program overview

The Certified People Sourcing Professional ( CPSP ) credential proves that the holder has the right sourcing skills to become a successful professional in the field of talent acquisition. Sourcing is about identifying and engaging with candidates, which is integral to the talent acquisition process.

If sourcing is your main job, this is one of the few credentials that tests search skills directly rather than recruiting theory. To get certified, participants must pass the CPSP exam, which requires the participant to demonstrate proficiency in the following six core skill groups:

- Exploratory research: Identifying companies to source from, determining which professional credentials or graduates of which schools to look for, exploring new markets.

- Google search: Learning how Google works, applying Boolean logic and search operators, using various specialty search products like patent, publication, and image searches.

- X-ray searching: Searching through social networks, contact databases, associations, and other sites that have user profiles and public pages.

- Cross-referencing information: Filling the gaps in the data (e.g., an email address, location, or employer) based on information from a social profile, phone number, or an image.

- Using LinkedIn: Understanding and using LinkedIn filters.

- Social search: Discovering and exploring social sites (e.g., Facebook, Instagram, Twitter, and Reddit). 

The Sourcing Certifications organization also has a Training Library with a wealth of materials on sourcing-related topics. They also offer custom training.

#### Details

- Whom it’s for : Individuals interested in becoming a sourcing professional or recruiters who want to improve their sourcing skills.

- Format : Online.

- Cost : $399 for the first exam attempt and $199 for recertification or each additional attempt.

### 4. Certified Personnel Consultant (NAPS)

#### Program overview

The National Association of Personnel Services (NAPS) offers the Certified Personnel Consultant (CPC) credential. To get certified, you must demonstrate your knowledge of employment laws and the legal implications of the work of direct-hire and temporary staffing professionals. The exam consists of 150 multiple-choice questions to test your knowledge of the following domains:

- Laws and regulations

- Candidate relations

- Client relations

- General industry practices.

The content also includes topics such as candidate sourcing, screening, interviewing, testing, placement, retention, coaching, client development, negotiation, contracts, service delivery, quality assurance, problem resolution, ethics, professionalism, industry trends, and terminology.

You must study the official NAPS Certification and Education Manual to prepare for the CPC exam. It contains information on all the exam domains, sample questions and answers, a glossary, and references. Online courses, webinars, podcasts, and articles are also available to supplement your studies.

#### Details

- Whom it’s for : Recruiting and HR professionals, as well as hiring managers involved in talent acquisition processes. 

- Format/duration : Online.

- Cost : $300 for members, $600 for non-members.

## How to become a certified recruiter

Becoming a certified recruiter takes a few weeks to a few months, depending on the credential. Most recruiter-specific certifications have no formal prerequisites, so the real work is choosing well and preparing. Follow these steps:

- Define your track: Decide which kind of recruiting you do now, or want to do next: in-house, agency, sourcing, or technical. Your track narrows the list quickly.

- Check eligibility and time: SHRM’s specialty credential and AIRS certifications don’t require prior certification, while HRCI’s PHR requires HR experience. Compare study hours against your workload.

- Set your budget: Add the program or exam fee to any renewal costs. If you’d like your employer to cover it, build a short business case tied to time-to-fill or quality of hire. Our guide on asking your employer to sponsor training can help you.

- Prepare and complete the assessment: Most programs end with an exam or a capstone project. Use the practice exams, templates, and case studies your program includes.

- Show your credential: Add it to your résumé and the licenses and certifications or education section of your LinkedIn profile, and talk about the skills it covers in interviews.

- Keep it current: AIRS certifications renew every two years, while SHRM and HRCI certifications renew every three years through recertification credits.
