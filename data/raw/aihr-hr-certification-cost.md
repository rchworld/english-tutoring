## Why should you get an HR certification?

Completing an HR certificate program can benefit both current professionals and those trying to break into the HR field in many ways, such as improving credibility with employers. For instance, many HR jobs (especially senior roles) require specific certifications, and employers tend to see certified professionals as more credible, which boosts employability.

HR certificate completion also demonstrates commitment to development by showing you’re serious about your professional growth and continuous improvement, which are crucial when preparing for future leadership roles. Certification can also boost your salary prospects and advance your career by making you more suitable for more senior positions like HRBP .

The right program helps you develop the knowledge, skills, and practical experience that enable you to contribute more to your organization and increase your confidence at work. Some programs even help you build your professional network through memberships and access to industry experts, industry events, and online HR communities.

How much does an HR certification cost? Program fees vary widely from one provider to another, with factors like membership, learning materials, exam format, and length of study all playing a role. Let’s explore some of the most popular HR certifications and their associated costs.

Exam fees for the SHRM-CP and SHRM-SCP are $399 for members and $499 for non-members, with early bird discounts offered as well. SHRM offers SHRM-CP and SHRM-SCP exams during two windows each year.

#### Recertification

Once certified, your certification cycle will end three years later, on the last day of your birth month. From this point, you will have two months to get recertified before your certification is revoked. The regular recertification processing fee is $165 for members and $210 for non-members; you must pay this fee before your recertification cycle ends.

If you miss this deadline, the late recertification fee is $240 for members and $285 for non-members. It applies only within the two-month window after your recertification cycle ends, and before your certification is revoked.

#### Grants and subsidies

The SHRM Foundation distributes over $200,000 in grants each year to SHRM members seeking their SHRM-CP or SHRM-SCP certification. These can help to cover the exam and Learning System fee for members. The SHRMA also offers a local chapter scholarship to one of it s members each year. Check out its website to determine your eligibility.

HRCI charges a $100 application fee per exam, and its exam fees vary depending on the credential — $300 for aPHR, $395 for PHR, and $495 for SPHR. HRCI waives the application fee for service members, veterans, and their spouses, and many of its credentials are eligible for VA reimbursement.

Some associations offer small discounts on HRCI exams; you can find out more on the HRCI promotions page .

#### Recertification

HRCI certifications have a three-year cycle. Its current recertification fees are $169 for one recertification and $50 for each additional recertification after that. You have 12 months after the end of each cycle to recertify. If your certification is suspended due to a late submission, you must pay a $100 late fee in addition to the existing recertification fees.

Once your certification enters suspended status, you have a 12-month grace period to complete your recertification requirements and pay the standard fees. Do note that you cannot verify your certification while it’s suspended.

## How do you choose the right HR certification?

With so many options available and different price points, how do you choose the right program to help you become HR certified ? It’s not just the HR certification cost that matters. Your career stage, aspirations, and preferred learning format should all inform your decision.

### Where are you in your career?

If you’re trying to break into HR or have just begun your HR career, we recommend opting for the aPHR (HRCI), TMI’s Talent Management Practitioner (TMP) Certification , or a targeted AIHR certificate program relevant to the area of HR you wish to enter. For example, if you want to be a Benefits Administrator, the Compensation & Benefits Certificate Program is perfect for you.

If you’re currently an experienced HR Generalist or looking to move into a business partner role, the SHRM-CP or PHR could be more beneficial. You could also consider an HCI skills program (for example, the Strategic HR Business Partner Certification ) or AIHR’s HR Business Partner 2.0 Certificate Program .

Those already in a senior or leadership role may find the SHRM-SCP, SPHR, CPTD ,and GTML HR certifications, as well as HCI’s strategic programs, more suitable for them.

### How do you prefer to learn?

Some HR certification providers offer exam-based credentials (e.g., the SHRM, HRCI, and ATD), while others provide program-based certificates (AIHR, HCI, and TMI ). The type you choose will depend on your learning style and career goals.

For instance, some prefer to memorize large amounts of information and complete exams that validate their mastery. Others find the pressure of exams overwhelming and like to build their skills through projects and written assignments. Consider what learning environment suits you best and choose an HR certificate program that aligns with your needs.

### What’s the best return on investment?

It’s essential not just to consider the one-off HR certification cost, but also any associated costs, including preparation, recertification, your time commitment, the recognition of the certification in your industry, and the transferability of the credits you’ll gain.

For instance, if you have a full-time 9-to-5 job and only have evenings or weekends available for studying, an accredited, self-paced online course that allows for flexible learning and provides 12-month access might be the best option. A good example is AIHR’s certificate programs, which are all online, self-paced, and accredited.

## How do you reduce or avoid HR certification costs altogether?

There are various ways for HR professionals to obtain subsidies or have their HR certification costs fully covered. Let’s explore these options.

- Employer funding: Many organizations offer tuition assistance and professional development budgets to help their employees upskill, which is tied to business outcomes.

- SHRM grants and chapter scholarships: The SHRM Foundation offers grants, and local chapters also provide reimbursements.

- HRCI military benefits: If you are a service member, veteran, or military spouse serving in the armed forces, your HRCI application fee is waived . There’s also VA reimbursement that could mean you pay nothing out of pocket.

- HCI membership savings: HCI offers 20% off all certification programs to Premium members .

- General learning platforms: If you lack the budget for an online HR certification, consider using edX financial assistance (for up to 90% off verified certificates) and limited LinkedIn Learning free access/trials to build skills and earn interim certificates. Although these are not SHRM/HRCI credentials, they can support your preparation for certification and aid in your professional development.

- Harvard discounts and financial assistance : Past participants can benefit from discounted enrollment in future courses, while learners working in nonprofit, military, and/or education fields can receive discounted rates on select programs. Different discounts apply to various courses on the edX platform, the GetSmarter platform, and Harvard online courses .



### To sum up

HR certification costs vary from one organization to another and increase in line with your seniority level, course comprehensiveness, and accreditation. However, you can reduce this cost through membership, grants and subsidies, or employer sponsorship.

Consider your career path and the HR certification that will be most valuable to you as you progress through the ranks. Remember to be realistic with your existing commitments, the time you can afford to invest, and the program that will best suit your lifestyle and career goals.
