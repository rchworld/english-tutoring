## Why get an HR certification?

More than experience and formal education, Human Resources is a field in which it’s critical to demonstrate how knowledgeable you are to employers. Staying up to date on employment laws and HR best practices can be challenging, yet it’s vital to staying relevant in the world of HR.

HR certification can support your long-term career success. SHRM reports that professionals who hold its certification earn salaries 14% to 15% higher than peers without the credential . If you’re starting a new career or venturing into a new specialty as an HR professional, an HR certification can help you get a head start.

Here are the key benefits of an HR certification:

- Improvement of knowledge, skills, and experience level

- Better job performance with the use of new skills

- Showcasing commitment and up-to-date knowledge to current and future employers

- Creating a competitive edge, increasing your value in the job market

- Building a future-proof career in HR.

The good news is that there are many ways to become HR certified , including convenient online HR certification programs. Most of the options in this article are available to HR professionals worldwide, and we highlight the ones that focus on a specific country or region.

## What HR certification should you get first?

The best HR certification to start with depends on where you are in your career, not on which credential is most prestigious. A certification that opens doors for a senior HR leader can be the wrong first move for someone two years into the field.

Keep in mind that a certification proves what you know, but it doesn’t teach you how to do the job. At every stage, it pays to pair it with learning that builds practical skills. Use the stages below to find what fits your experience now, then plan your next step as your role grows.

### Entry level (0 to two years in HR)

If you’re new to HR or moving from another field, start with a credential that doesn’t require years of HR experience. HRCI’s aPHR has no experience requirement, which makes it a practical first step for recent graduates and career changers. The SHRM-CP is also open to you, since SHRM requires no degree or experience to take the exam. Either one signals foundational knowledge to employers while you build a track record.

What employers will notice just as much is whether you can do the work. Look for a structured, beginner-level program, such as AIHR’s HR Coordinator or HR Generalist Certificate Programs , that doesn’t require prior HR experience, covers the full employee lifecycle, and lets you practice real tasks through assignments and simulations. Ready-to-use templates, such as onboarding checklists and policy templates, will help you hit the ground running in your first role.

### Mid-level (two to five years in HR)

At this stage, you’re running HR processes and advising managers, so the two strongest options are the PHR and the SHRM-CP. Choose the PHR if your work is operational and U.S. compliance sits at the center of your role. Choose the SHRM-CP if you want a competency-based credential. Let your day-to-day focus decide.

This is also the stage to specialize. Choose a program in your area of focus, such as AIHR’s People Analytics , Strategic Talent Acquisition , or HR Business Partner Certificate Program s, that’s taught by practitioners and has you work through real-world case studies and projects. If you already hold a certification, check whether the program earns recertification credits, so the same learning builds new skills and keeps your credential active.

### Senior level (five or more years in HR)

If you shape strategy, lead a function, or sit close to the executive team, move to a senior credential. The SPHR from HRCI and the SHRM-SCP from SHRM are both built for strategic HR work and senior-level positions.

Senior roles also call for skills that a knowledge exam can’t fully cover, such as leading AI adoption, consulting with the business, and driving HR transformation. Look for applied learning that’s updated as the field changes, uses realistic business scenarios, and ends with an assessment you must pass to earn the certificate.

### 2. Professional in Human Resources (HRCI)

The HR Certification Institute (HRCI) is a well-known certification body for HR professionals. Its Professional in Human Resources (PHR) validates technical and operational HR knowledge, grounded in U.S. laws and regulations. With certification levels in the senior and global areas of HR management careers, it’s possible to start your journey and earn advanced certification as you gain on-the-job experience and increased levels of responsibility.

Candidates can sit for the associate level certification ( aPHR ), then advance to the PHR, SPHR, and GPHR. You can take the exams at a Pearson VUE testing center or online at home or in the office through OnVUE. The PHR exam takes two hours and includes 90 scored questions.

- Who it’s for: HR professionals focused on implementing programs and supporting day-to-day operations. The aPHR , SPHR, and GPHR cover other career stages.

- Eligibility requirements: A master’s degree with one year of professional-level HR experience, a bachelor’s degree with two years, or four years of professional-level HR experience.

- Cost: $495 (including a $100 application fee).

Keep in mind: The PHR is an exam that validates knowledge rather than a course that teaches skills, and its content is grounded in U.S. laws. Fees aren’t refunded once your application is approved, and you must earn 60 recertification credits every three years or retake the exam.

### 3. SHRM Certified Professional (SHRM-CP), SHRM Senior Certified Professional (SHRM-SCP)

The Society for Human Resource Management (SHRM) offers competency-based HR certifications, based on the SHRM Body of Applied Skills and Knowledge (SHRM BASK). The SHRM-CP focuses on HR operations, policy execution, and employee support, while the SHRM-SCP focuses on strategic HR, policy development, and enterprise planning.

SHRM also offers specialty credentials in areas such as AI+HI, people analytics, workplace investigations, and talent acquisition, plus a California Law HR credential for professionals working in that state.

- Who it’s for: The SHRM-CP suits HR professionals in day-to-day operational roles or those pursuing a career in HR. The SHRM-SCP is for senior HR professionals leading strategic initiatives.

- Eligibility requirements: None for SHRM-CP; at least three years in strategic HR, or holding the SHRM-CP for three years, for SHRM-SCP.

- Cost : $350 to $499, depending on membership status and whether you apply by the early-bird deadline. SHRM’s official prep options are priced separately.

Keep in mind: You can only test during SHRM’s two testing windows each year, at a Prometric test center, and you need to apply within the matching application period. Like the PHR, it validates what you know rather than teaching new skills.

### 4. Talent Acquisition Specialty Credential (SHRM)

SHRM’s Talent Acquisition Specialty Credential is a focused option alongside its SHRM-CP and SHRM-SCP certifications. Rather than a broad HR exam, it combines an instructor-led program, Talent Acquisition: Creating Your Organization’s Strategy, with three eLearning courses on global hiring, inclusion and diversity in talent acquisition, and virtual talent acquisition.

The credential is designed to validate specialist expertise for people who spend most of their time on hiring. After completing all components and passing a final knowledge assessment, you earn 22 professional development credits (PDCs), a certificate of achievement, and a digital badge.

- Who it’s for: Recruiters, talent acquisition specialists, and HR generalists who want to formalize and deepen their hiring expertise.

- Eligibility requirements: Not listed on the credential page.

- Cost: Not listed on the credential page; check SHRM’s store before you enroll.

Keep in mind: It covers talent acquisition only, so it won’t build your broader HR skills, and it ends with a knowledge assessment rather than a hands-on project.

### 5. CIPD Qualifications (Levels 3, 5, and 7)

The Chartered Institute of Personnel and Development (CIPD) is the professional body for HR and people development, and it describes its qualifications as recognized by employers as a global standard. They’re structured across three levels that match different career stages: Level 3 (Foundation Certificate) for those starting out, Level 5 (Associate Diploma) for practitioners ready to grow, and Level 7 (Advanced Diploma) for experienced professionals moving into strategic roles.

CIPD qualifications are taught by external study centers (online, in a classroom, or both) and awarded by the CIPD. Each center sets its own fees. A Foundation qualification typically takes 8–12 months, an Associate Diploma 12–16 months, and an Advanced Diploma 16–24 months. You join as a Student Member while you study. Completing Level 5 or 7 leads to Associate Membership, and with enough experience you may progress to Chartered Membership.

- Who it’s for: HR and L&D professionals who want an academically structured qualification matched to their career stage.

- Eligibility requirements: Level 3 suits beginners or people in support roles, Level 5 is for existing people professionals, and Level 7 is for experienced professionals. CIPD student membership applies while studying.

- Cost: In the U.K., typically £1,300 to £2,300 for Level 3, £1,600 to £3,600 for Level 5, and £3,000 to £7,000 for Level 7, depending on the study center. Student membership is £118 a year, plus a one-off £40 joining fee.

Keep in mind: It’s a long commitment, from 8 months for Level 3 to as long as two years for Level 7, and the qualifications are mapped to U.K. and Irish qualification frameworks.

### 6. Strategic HR Leadership (HCI)

The Human Capital Institute (HCI) focuses on aligning human capital with business strategy, typically for HR leaders. Its Strategic HR Leadership (SHRL) certification helps you move from HR practitioner to strategic people leader, covering how to assess your team leadership skills, design teams around strategy, and influence outcomes. You get a participant guide and toolkit, and the program earns 13.5 HCI, HRCI, and SHRM credits and 12 ATD credits.

The program runs as a live, two-day virtual class on set dates. Learners must attend all sessions, complete all classwork, and pass a multiple-choice exam with a score of 80% or higher to earn the certification.

- Who it’s for: Those who are new to HR leadership roles, want to be people leaders, or need to improve their team’s performance.

- Eligibility requirements: None listed on the program page.

- Cost: $1,995 (20% off with HCI Premium or Corporate Membership)

Keep in mind: You need to attend two full days live on fixed dates (9:00 AM to 5:00 PM U.S. Eastern time), and the content focuses on leading HR teams rather than core HR processes.

### 7. Certified Human Resources Professional (HRPA)

The Human Resources Professionals Association (HRPA) in Ontario, Canada, offers the Certified Human Resources Professional (CHRP) designation, which validates an HR practitioner’s foundational knowledge, workplace readiness, and ethical standards.

You must be an HRPA registrant and complete four steps in order: coursework in nine HR subject areas, the CHRP Knowledge Exam (175 multiple-choice questions), the CHRP Employment Law Exam, and the online Job Ready Program on professionalism and ethics.

- Who it’s for: Early-career HR professionals handling essential, day-to-day HR tasks.

- Eligibility requirements: HRPA registration in good standing and completion of the coursework requirement (a grade of at least 65% in each of the nine subject areas and an overall average of 70%), or qualifying through the Alternate Route.

- Cost : CA$350 for the CHRP Knowledge Exam and CA$300 for the CHRP Employment Law Exam (plus HST), in addition to coursework and HRPA membership fees.

Keep in mind: The employment law exam covers Ontario law, and you must keep your HRPA membership and meet ongoing professional development requirements to hold the designation.

### 8. Talent Management Practitioner (TMI)

The Talent Management Practitioner (TMP) certification offered by the Talent Management Institute is a foundational credential for those breaking into talent management, built on TMI’s Universal Knowledge Framework (TMI-UKF). It tests your knowledge of the principles, theories, techniques, and tools used in talent management through an online, digitally proctored exam.

The TMP is the first rung of TMI’s three-level ladder, followed by the Senior Talent Management Practitioner (STMP) and the Global Talent Management Leader (GTML). It comes with a digital badge, and every candidate receives the TMI Resource Box, which includes the institute’s practitioner handbook, plus practice tests on the myTMI dashboard.

- Who it’s for: HR professionals one or two years into their careers, and students about to graduate, who want to move into talent management.

- Eligibility requirements: One of six tracks, for example, a bachelor’s degree in HRM, business, or management plus six months of experience in an HR sub-function, or a bachelor’s in another discipline plus two years. A QualiFly route is available to students at TMI-recognized institutions and to holders of recognized HR certifications.

- Cost : $675, which covers one exam attempt, the Resource Box, the digital badge, and shipping.

Keep in mind: The fee covers one exam attempt and doesn’t include training from preparation providers; refunds are only available within 24 hours of payment.



### 9. Certified Professional in Talent Development (ATD)

The Association for Talent Development (ATD) offers the Certified Professional in Talent Development (CPTD). It measures your knowledge and skill application across the breadth of talent development capabilities.

The CPTD is a computer-based exam that you take during one of ATD’s testing windows, which you choose when you apply. ATD offers optional preparation tools and resources, which aren’t included in the exam fee.

- Who it’s for: HR and talent development professionals who want to continue building their talent development capabilities.

- Eligibility requirements: Must have five years of related work experience and deep knowledge of employee talent development principles.

- Cost : $1,099 for ATD members and $1,500 for non-members (exam fee).

Keep in mind: It requires five years of experience, exam fees are non-refundable, and preparation materials cost extra.

### 10. Fundamental Payroll Certification and Certified Payroll Professional (PayrollOrg)

PayrollOrg (formerly the American Payroll Association) offers the two leading payroll credentials in the U.S. The Fundamental Payroll Certification (FPC) is an entry-level certification that covers core paycheck calculation, federal tax compliance, and payroll systems and processes. The Certified Payroll Professional (CPP) is the advanced credential for experienced HR practitioners, testing extensive expertise in compliance, administration, accounting, and payroll management.

Both are exam-based and recognized as the standard for payroll roles within and adjacent to HR. Both credentials are valid for three years and require recertification credit hours to maintain.

- Who it’s for: Payroll specialists, compensation professionals, and HR generalists who handle payroll and want a dedicated credential in the field.

- Eligibility requirements: None for the FPC, but the CPP requires meeting one of PayrollOrg ’s experience criteria (e.g., three years of payroll work in the preceding five years).

- Cost: Check with the provider

Keep in mind: Exam questions are based on U.S. federal laws and regulations, and you can only test during the set exam windows.

### 11. Human Resource Information Professional (IHRIM)

The International Association for Human Resource Information Management (IHRIM) offers the Human Resource Information Professional (HRIP) certification, which assesses the knowledge and competencies of HR technology professionals. The exam has 90 multiple-choice questions across five domains, from technology strategy to HR systems operation, and you can take it with live online proctoring or at a Meazure Learning test center.

- Who it’s for: Experienced HR technology professionals working at the intersection of people, processes, and technology.

- Eligibility requirements: At least five years of experience in HR technology, with working knowledge across the five knowledge domains.

- Cost: $445 for the exam only (non-members) plus a $75 application fee, or $1,320 for the full bundle with the exam review course, practice exam, and study guide ($1,100 for IHRIM members).

### 12. Certified HR Manager (HR University)

HR University offers the Certified HR Manager (CHRM) as an online, self-paced program aimed at building manager-level HR skills. Delivered as an on-demand course of more than 90 video lessons, it covers the HR management remit, from strategic HRM and HR analytics to labor relations, workplace safety, and global HR.

The program uses a practical application, with 30-plus case studies, analytical activities, quizzes, and interviews with working HR professionals, and is structured to be completed at roughly four hours a week over about 10 weeks. On finishing, you complete a certification test and receive a digital badge and e-certificate with a verification link. SHRM also recognizes HR University to offer PDCs for SHRM-CP or SHRM-SCP recertification.

- Who it’s for: Aspiring and current HR managers, career changers, and HR professionals who want a practical, flexible credential covering the fundamentals of HR management.

- Eligibility requirements: None listed.

- Cost : $199 for the single CHRM certificate program, or $499 (shown as a sale price) for full access to HR University’s six certificate programs. A 30-day money-back guarantee applies.

### Eligibility at a glance

AIHR Certificate Programs have no eligibility requirements. Program levels range from foundational to expert, so you can choose one that matches your experience. Entry-level programs, such as HR Coordinator, don’t require any previous HR experience.

Certifications differ because most require you to have experience before you can apply. HRCI’s aPHR has no HR experience requirement, but the PHR requires one to four years of professional HR experience, depending on your education, and the SPHR requires four to seven years. SHRM requires no degree or experience to sit the SHRM-CP, while the SHRM-SCP requires three or more years in strategic HR or holding the SHRM-CP for three years. Always confirm the current criteria on the certifying body’s site before you apply, since requirements change.

### Recertification: The ongoing cost

Certifications aren’t a one-time cost. SHRM requires 60 professional development credits (PDCs) every three years and a recertification processing fee of $165 for members or $210 for non-members. HRCI requires 45 recertification credits for the aPHR and 60 for the PHR and SPHR over three years. With either body, you can earn these credits through approved learning activities rather than retaking the exam.
