## What makes an HR certification “easy”?

When HR professionals search for easy HR certifications, they rarely mean “low value” or “effortless.” They usually mean accessible.

An easier HR certification typically has lower entry requirements, a manageable scope, and a clear learning path. It is often designed for people who are new to HR, changing careers, or building foundational knowledge before specializing . That is why credentials like HRCI’s aPHR and aPHRi appear so often in beginner conversations: both require no prior HR experience and only a high school diploma or equivalent.

SHRM-CP is also more accessible than many people assume, as SHRM states that candidates do not need a degree or prior HR experience to apply, even though basic HR knowledge is recommended. Certificate programs often don’t have eligibility requirements. For example, anyone can start an AIHR Certificate Program, so you don’t have to prove experience or education before you learn.

Accessibility also depends on format. Online or year-round options make it easier for busy HR professionals. HRCI lets you apply for aPHR and aPHRi any time, and you can take the exams at a Pearson VUE center or online.

The SHRM-CP, by contrast, is offered in two testing windows a year and must be taken in person at a Prometric test center . That doesn’t make it harder to qualify for, but it does make the process less flexible. AIHR’s certificate programs offer more flexible learning opportunities: they’re 100% online and self-paced, with no testing window to wait for and no test center to travel to, and you get 12 months of access to finish.

Pass rates are also important to consider. HRCI reports a 71% pass rate for the aPHR as of December 31, 2025, and SHRM’s most recent published SHRM-CP pass rate is 68%, for the May to July 2025 window. The gap is not large, but what makes the aPHR easier is its shorter exam and lower entry bar.

Ease isn’t the only thing to weigh, though. A certification validates what you already know, while a certificate program builds new skills. AIHR’s certificate programs teach through Tell-Show-Do-Apply video lessons, let you practice on real-world case studies and roleplays, and give you ready-to-use templates for the job.

You earn your digital certificate by passing a capstone exam, which you can retake as often as you need. This makes it more accessible, and you can do the work after completing the program. role.

## Certification vs. certificate program: Which one is the right choice for you?

A certification usually means you meet a standard set by an external credentialing body. In practice, that often means passing an exam and maintaining the credential over time. Credentials such as aPHR, aPHRi, SHRM-CP, and PHR are certifications offered by established credentialing bodies.

A certificate program is different. For example, AIHR Certificate Programs are structured, start-to-finish learning journeys designed to build practical skills. You don’t earn a designation through these programs. You earn a digital certificate when you pass the program’s capstone exam and, more importantly, build skills you can use on the job.

This difference is important. If you want external credibility and a recognized exam-based credential, certification is likely the right path. If you want to learn how to do something better, like run HR operations , recruit, or use AI in HR, a certificate program may be a better first step.

### 2. aPHR (HRCI)

Best for: Beginners with little or no HR experience

The Associate Professional in Human Resources (aPHR) is one of the clearest starting points in HR. HRCI says it is designed for people starting their HR journey or exploring the field, and it requires no HR experience. You only need a high school diploma or equivalent to apply.

This certification is on the easy list because it is designed for beginners. It gives you a broad understanding of HR and shows that you know the basics. It is a good first credential for career changers, HR assistants, students, and recent graduates.

You may want to skip this if you already have solid HR experience and are looking for a credential that shows deeper specialization or strategic skills. Pick this option if you want a formal HR certification and a well-known way to start your career.

### 3. aPHRi (HRCI)

Best for: International beginners who want a foundational HR credential outside the U.S.

The aPHRi is the international version of the aPHR. HRCI positions it for people starting their HR journey and says it requires no prior HR experience, with the same high school diploma or equivalent baseline. Its purpose is similar to the aPHR, but it fits better for HR roles outside the U.S., and is one of the easiest HR certifications if you want a formal starting point with a global focus.

It’s a good fit for career changers, coordinators, junior HR staff, and new professionals working internationally. It’s less suitable if you want a U.S.-specific credential or already have enough experience for a more advanced certification. Choose this if you want an entry-level HR certification, and your context is international rather than U.S.-focused.

### 4. SHRM-CP

Best for: People who want a broad, established HR credential with strong market recognition.

SHRM-CP is not the easiest exam here, but it is more accessible than many expect. SHRM says it is for people doing HR work, students, and those starting an HR career. You do not need a degree or HR experience to apply, but basic HR knowledge is recommended.

This makes SHRM-CP a good choice for early-career professionals who want a broad credential and are ready to study. It is a strong fit for aspiring generalists, coordinators moving up, and those who want a credential recognized by many employers.

You may want to skip this for now if you do not want to prepare for an exam yet or if you want to focus on building one practical skill, like recruiting or using AI in HR. Pick this if you want a broad, well-known certification and are ready for a more structured exam process.

### 5. PHR (HRCI)

Best for: HR professionals ready for a more tactical, U.S.-focused credential

The Professional in Human Resources (PHR) is a more advanced option than aPHR, but it can still be an accessible next step for professionals who already have some HR experience. HRCI says the PHR is designed for HR professionals who are focused on program implementation and have a tactical and operational orientation to HR management.

This means it is not as beginner-friendly as aPHR, aPHRi, or SHRM-CP. Still, it can make sense for HR professionals who want a respected U.S.-focused certification and are ready to move beyond entry-level knowledge.

Skip this if you are completely new to HR or want the lowest-barrier option. Choose this if you already have some hands-on HR experience and want a more established next-step credential.



## Tips for choosing the right easy HR certification

The right HR certification is not always the one with the lowest barrier to entry. It is the one that fits your current experience, the skill you want to build, and the role you want next. Use these tips to narrow your options and choose a path that supports your career.

### Start with the skill you want to build

Before you compare certifications, get specific about what you want to improve. Are you trying to build a broad HR foundation, or do you want to get better at one area like recruiting, HR analytics, compensation, DEIB, or organizational development? The clearer your goal is, the easier it becomes to choose the right learning path.

### Decide whether you need a certification or a certificate program

This is one of the most important choices to make early on. A certification is usually the better fit if you want a formal credential that validates your knowledge and supports your professional credibility. A certificate program may be the better option if you want to build practical skills, test a specialization, or start learning without committing to exam prep.

A simple way to think about it is this:

- Choose a certification if you want a recognized credential

- Choose a certificate program if you want structured, practical skill-building

- Choose based on what will help you most in your current role or next move.

### Check the official eligibility requirements before you commit

This matters more than most people think. aPHR and aPHRi require no prior HR experience. SHRM-CP does not require previous HR experience either, but SHRM recommends basic HR knowledge. PHR, by contrast, is better suited to professionals who already have hands-on HR experience.

Before you invest time and money, make sure the requirements match where you are right now. A credential may sound accessible at first glance, but still be a poor fit if it expects more experience than you have.

### Consider which options you can complete online

While you can get HR certified online, not every exam allows it. HRCI’s aPHR, aPHRi, and PHR can be taken remotely through Pearson VUE’s OnVUE online proctoring, but the SHRM-CP can’t. It requires you to take the exam in person at a Prometric test center.

Certificate programs are the most flexible option. AIHR’s programs run fully online and are 100% self-paced. They end with a capstone project, which is taken online rather than at a proctored test center, so you never have to book a test slot or travel to a center.

### Compare the total cost, not just the fee

The exam fee is only part of what you’ll pay for a certification. The aPHR costs $400 (a $300 exam fee and non-refundable $100 application fee) before study materials. HRCI offers a variety of self-study exam preparation options , ranging in price from $50 to $880.

The SHRM-CP costs $399 to $499, depending on membership status. SHRM also offers student pricing from $150 if you meet its student eligibility requirements. Otherwise, it offers a SHRM Certification Prep System that costs $1,130 to $1,330, with a $310 discount for members.

A certificate program bundles the learning into one price. A single AIHR Certificate Program costs $1,125, including 12 months of access, expert community support, and every template in the program, with a 30-day money-back guarantee.
